# RallyHub marketing plan, v1

Stonewire Technologies · August 2026 · draft for partner review

This is a first version written against the August 2026 competitive brief and the
product as it actually stands today. It is opinionated on purpose. The parts most
likely to change after the partner meeting are marked **Decide**.

---

## 1. The one-sentence strategy

**Sell RallyHub to event agencies as the platform they can put their own name on, starting
in the markets where no platform competitor has a foothold, and use those first clients to
build the proof we currently do not have.**

Everything below follows from that sentence.

---

## 2. Positioning

### The wedge

Our only uncontested advantage is **true white-label multi-tenancy**: an agency runs
events on their own subdomain, under their own logo, with our name removed entirely.

This matters more than it sounds, because it is the one thing our closest feature
competitor structurally cannot copy. TeamingLabs licenses its software to Spanish agencies
while bidding against those same agencies for the same accounts, served from
`app.teaminglabs.com`. Their customers are visibly running a competitor's product.

**Positioning statement**

> For event companies that run team-building as a business, RallyHub is the live event
> platform they can sell as their own, because it is white-label to the domain and we never
> appear in front of their client.

**The line:** *The platform, not the agency. We never show up at your client's event.*

### What we lead with, in order

1. **White-label.** Their brand, their domain, our name gone.
2. **The whole event in one tool.** Quests, quizzes, puzzles and music bingo in one run,
   on one leaderboard. Rivals do one genre well.
3. **Reuse economics.** Build the format once, rebrand per client, new event in ten minutes.
4. **The live control room.** One screen runs the room.
5. **The store.** Points become physical items. Rare, though no longer unique.

### What we stop saying

- "No other platform does this" about the store. TeamingLabs has it, and deeper.
  *(Already corrected on the live site.)*
- Anything implying we invented the facilitator control room. espoto sells one and
  charges double for it.

### Positioning by segment

| Segment | What they buy | Lead message |
|---|---|---|
| **Event agencies** (primary) | Margin and reusability | White-label + build once, sell forever |
| **Companies** running their own days | Not embarrassing themselves | Ready-made formats, no production crew |
| **Venues and hosts** (quiz nights) | Weekly repeatability | Set it up once, run it every Thursday |

Agencies are the plan. The other two are self-serve overflow, not a sales motion.

## 3. Market sequencing

**Do not launch nine countries at once.** With a small team and no case studies, nine
simultaneous launches means nine half-launches. The competitive map says the markets are
not equally winnable.

| Market | Competitive reality | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Malta** | No platform incumbent. Small, tight, word of mouth travels | **Phase 1** |
| **Bulgaria** | mooveTEAM localised but stale. Founder speaks the language | **Phase 1** |
| **Poland** | mooveTEAM localised; no strong local platform | **Phase 2** |
| **Netherlands** | No local incumbent; high English fluency lowers localisation cost | **Phase 2** |
| **Lithuania, Latvia** | Loquiz is regionally close and strong, but has no live layer | **Phase 3** |
| **France** | No researched incumbent; needs full localisation and a native rep | **Phase 3** |
| **Spain** | TeamingLabs owns the search and relationships; mooveTEAM is Spanish; FUNDAE subsidy we cannot match | **Phase 4, white-label pitch only** |
| **Germany** | espoto and Actionbound home turf, both strong | **Phase 4, or never head-on** |

### Phasing

**Phase 1 — Prove it (months 1 to 3): Malta + Bulgaria**
Goal is not revenue. Goal is **three named reference clients, three case studies, and the
first ten reviews.** Everything in Phase 2 is blocked without them.

**Phase 2 — Scale the motion (months 4 to 8): + Poland, Netherlands**
First markets with a dedicated rep each. Full site localisation in Polish and Dutch.
Goal: prove the rep model repeats.

**Phase 3 — Widen (months 9 to 14): + Lithuania, Latvia, France**

**Phase 4 — The hard ones (month 15+): Spain, Germany**
Enter only with white-label as the entire pitch, targeting agencies that already resent
buying from a competitor.

**Decide:** whether Malta or Bulgaria is genuinely first, and whether we have a rep for
each Phase 2 market before committing.

## 4. Fixing three gaps before we scale

These are marketing problems even though two are product work. Phase 2 underperforms
without them.

### 4.1 No social proof (most urgent)

We have zero reviews, zero client logos, zero case studies. Loquiz has 101 Capterra
reviews at 4.7 and adds more weekly. A buyer comparing us finds nothing.

- List on **Capterra, G2 and GetApp**. espoto, mooveTEAM and Actionbound are all absent,
  so the category shelf is genuinely half empty.
- Ask every Phase 1 client for a review as part of the engagement, not as an afterthought.
- **Target: 10 reviews before Phase 2 opens.**

### 4.2 The slowest funnel in the category

espoto, Loquiz and Actionbound all let a buyer start in under a minute. We require booking
a demo and waiting.

- Add a **free build-and-preview tier**: create an account, build an event, preview every
  screen, run a demo event with a handful of devices. Pay only to run a real one.
- mooveTEAM's "free to build, pay when you sell" is the single sharpest weapon aimed at
  our monthly floor. This neutralises it.
- Keep the demo calendar as the agency path. Self-serve is for the other two segments.

### 4.3 No offline play

Five competitors cache maps and media. We are realtime browser dependent, which fails at a
rural offsite. **Decide:** build a cached mode, or explicitly position for indoor and
connected venues and stop bidding on outdoor work. Doing neither loses deals quietly.

## 5. Channels

Ranked by expected return for our situation. No paid brand advertising at this budget.

### 5.1 Direct outreach by country reps (primary)

This is the plan's engine. Everything else feeds it.

- **Target list per country:** every event agency, DMC and team-building company. Realistically
  50 to 200 per market. Sources: Google Maps, national event-industry associations, LinkedIn,
  conference exhibitor lists, competitor partner pages (mooveTEAM publishes 17+ partners).
- **The pitch is the white-label wedge**, not features.
- **Marketing supplies:** localised one-pager, the competitive battlecard, a 90-second demo
  video, an email sequence in each language, and a bookable calendar per rep.
- **Cadence:** email, follow-up, LinkedIn, call. Reps report replies and objections back
  monthly so messaging improves.

### 5.2 SEO and comparison content

espoto and Loquiz both out-rank us on everything. We will not beat them on head terms
quickly, but comparison and long-tail intent is winnable.

- **Comparison pages:** "RallyHub vs Loquiz", "vs espoto", "vs mooveTEAM", "vs Actionbound".
  These convert because the buyer is already deciding. Be scrupulously fair — the research
  in the competitive brief is accurate enough to publish nearly as-is.
- **Category pages per market:** "team building software Poland", "corporate event platform
  Netherlands", plus city pages for the largest three cities per market. TeamingLabs runs
  40+ city pages and it works.
- **Use-case pages:** conference icebreaker, company away day, school trip, quiz night,
  Christmas party. These are the buying occasions people actually search.
- **Cadence:** one substantial piece a month. Consistency beats volume at our size.

### 5.3 AI-assistant visibility (AEO)

TeamingLabs publishes a detailed `llms.txt` briefing assistants on how to describe them,
including a section on what not to invent. It is a real and under-contested channel.

- Publish `llms.txt` and a fuller version: what RallyHub is, who it is for, the game types,
  pricing structure, which markets we serve, and what not to fabricate.
- **Roughly a day of work.** Highest effort-to-visibility ratio on this list.

### 5.4 Review sites as a channel, not a checkbox

Capterra and G2 are where comparison happens. Being absent means being excluded.
See 4.1. Treat the listing as a landing page: full feature list, real screenshots, pricing.

### 5.5 Partnerships and networks

- National event-industry associations in each market: membership, directory listing,
  occasionally a speaking slot.
- **Do what TeamingLabs does:** they run an annual live showcase where agencies play the
  games as participants. Experiential selling beats a slide deck for this product.
  A small version — one afternoon, ten agencies, one city — is affordable and repeatable.

### 5.6 LinkedIn

The only social channel worth the time; it is where agency owners and HR buyers are.
Founder-led posts about how events actually run, plus reps sharing in their own language.
Not a broadcast brand account.

### 5.7 Paid, deliberately small

Only after comparison pages exist and convert. Search ads on competitor and category terms
in one market as a test, capped. If a test market cannot be made to work organically first,
paid will not rescue it.

## 6. Localisation

Our stated advantage: the site and platform translated per market, reps operating in the
local language. Competitors are weaker here — espoto is DE/EN only, Loquiz is English-first,
Actionbound is DE/EN. mooveTEAM is the exception with six languages.

**Order of translation** (follows the market phasing):

1. English (done) — the default and the fallback everywhere
2. Bulgarian, Maltese-market English (Phase 1)
3. Polish, Dutch (Phase 2)
4. Lithuanian, Latvian, French (Phase 3)
5. Spanish, German (Phase 4)

**What gets translated, in priority order:** marketing site → the player-facing app
(participants must never see a language they do not read) → the admin panel → support docs
→ email sequences and sales material.

**Rule:** never machine-translate the player experience. A participant hitting broken
Bulgarian mid-game costs the agency their client. Reps or paid native speakers review
everything player-facing.

## 7. The funnel, end to end

| Stage | Agency path | Self-serve path |
|---|---|---|
| **Discover** | Rep outreach, comparison pages, association directories, LinkedIn | Search, review sites, AI assistants |
| **Evaluate** | Live demo booked via calendar, run by the country rep in their language | Free build-and-preview tier |
| **Convince** | Case study from a comparable agency, white-label demo on their own subdomain, pricing maths against their current tool | Pricing page, reviews |
| **Buy** | Rep closes; first event often run alongside the client | Self-serve checkout |
| **Expand** | Second and third events, more devices, branding removal add-on | Upgrade prompts |
| **Advocate** | Case study, review, referral to other agencies | Review request after third event |

**The one demo rule:** never present slides. Build an event in front of them, branded as
one of their clients, and let them play it on their phone. Our own page already promises
this; the sales motion should honour it.

## 8. Twelve-month calendar

### Months 1 to 3 — Phase 1, prove it
- Ship the free build-and-preview tier
- List on Capterra, G2, GetApp; publish `llms.txt`
- Build the target list for Malta and Bulgaria
- Localise site and player app into Bulgarian
- Write the battlecard and the localised one-pager
- Publish the four comparison pages
- **Close three reference clients; produce three case studies; collect ten reviews**

### Months 4 to 8 — Phase 2, scale the motion
- Recruit and onboard reps for Poland and the Netherlands
- Localise into Polish and Dutch
- One content piece a month; city pages for the top three cities per market
- First live showcase afternoon in one city
- Decide and act on offline play
- **Target: repeatable rep motion, first ten paying agencies**

### Months 9 to 14 — Phase 3, widen
- Lithuania, Latvia, France: reps, localisation, association memberships
- Small paid search test in the best-performing Phase 2 market
- Second showcase event
- **Target: proof that the model works without founder involvement in every deal**

### Month 15+ — Phase 4, the hard markets
- Spain and Germany, white-label pitch only, targeting agencies already buying from
  a competitor-owned platform

## 9. What we measure

Vanity metrics are banned. These are the ones that predict revenue.

**Leading**
- Qualified demos booked per rep per month
- Demo-to-first-event conversion rate
- Free-tier signups that build a complete event (the real activation moment)
- Review count and average rating
- Comparison-page sessions and their conversion to demo

**Lagging**
- Paying agencies per market
- Events run per agency per quarter (the true health metric: agencies that run one event never come back)
- Revenue per market
- Devices per event, since it drives the per-event fee

**Instrumentation:** we cannot improve what we do not track. Analytics on the marketing site
and activation events in the product need to exist before Phase 2, or Phase 2 is guesswork.

**Review cadence:** monthly numbers, quarterly strategy, and a re-run of the competitive
brief every quarter. espoto ships monthly — if they add a physical store or white-label,
our combination advantage collapses to a single feature and this plan needs rewriting.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| espoto adds white-label or a physical store | Medium | Quarterly competitive re-check; deepen the store (stock, limits, unlock-next) |
| Loquiz's review lead becomes unassailable | High if we do nothing | Start collecting reviews in Phase 1, not Phase 2 |
| Reps do not produce without close management | Medium | Small Phase 1 test before committing to four markets |
| Nine languages outrun our ability to support them | High | Phased localisation; English fallback everywhere |
| No offline play loses outdoor deals | Medium | Decide in Phase 2 |
| Spain and Germany stay closed | High | Accept it; they are Phase 4 for a reason |
| Founder capacity is the real constraint | High | Sequence markets; do not parallelise |

## 12. If only three things happen

1. **Get to three case studies and ten reviews.** Nothing else scales without them.
2. **Ship the free build-and-preview tier.** It removes the single sharpest objection a
   competitor can raise against our pricing.
3. **Make white-label the whole pitch.** It is the only thing we have that no competitor
   can answer, and our closest rival structurally never will.
