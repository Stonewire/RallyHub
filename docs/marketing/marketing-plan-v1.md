# RallyHub marketing plan, v1

Stonewire Technologies · August 2026 · draft for partner review

Written against the August 2026 competitive brief, the product as it stands today, and
Rumen's answers on budget, goals, capacity and markets. Opinionated on purpose. Items that
need a decision in the meeting are marked **Decide**.

---

## 0. The constraints this plan is built for

| Input | Reality |
|---|---|
| Marketing budget | **Under €500 a month**, excluding founder time |
| Twelve-month goal | **Revenue** |
| Country sales reps | **None yet** |
| Founder time for sales and marketing | **20+ hours a week** |
| Existing clients usable as proof | **One or two, informally** |
| Offline play | **Wait and see** |
| Target markets | Malta, Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Bulgaria, Romania, Greece, Croatia, Cyprus |

Two of these are in direct tension, and the plan resolves it in section 3: **nine markets on
under €500 a month with no reps is not a plan, it is nine half-launches.** Everything else
follows from taking that seriously.

## 1. The single biggest finding

**We already have the free self-serve path our competitors use against us. We just never
mention it.**

On the Pay Per Event plan a prospect can, today, with no subscription and no sales contact:
sign up, build games, build a full event, brand it, and **run it live for free** as a Demo
event. The database is explicit: demo status does not bill. They pay €199 only when they
switch an event to Active and run it for real.

That is stronger than mooveTEAM's "free to build, pay only when you sell", which is the
sharpest attack any competitor has on our pricing. They can build for free. Our users can
build **and actually run a live event** for free.

Meanwhile the homepage says "Book a demo" seven times and never once says "start free".

**This is a marketing failure, not a product gap, and it is the cheapest fix in the plan.**

**Actions**
- Add a second CTA beside Book a demo: **"Start free, no card"**, linked to registration.
- Rewrite the pricing section to lead with the free path: build, brand and run a demo event
  at no cost; pay per event only when you go live.
- Say it explicitly on the comparison pages, because this is the exact objection mooveTEAM
  raises.
- **Decide:** whether free demo events stay uncapped, or get a soft limit (for example three)
  before an upgrade prompt.

## 2. Strategy in one sentence

**Sell to event agencies as the platform they can put their own name on, prove it in three
concentrated markets with the founder as the first salesperson, and let revenue from those
markets pay for the reps who open the rest.**

### Positioning

Our only uncontested advantage is **true white-label multi-tenancy**: an agency runs events
on their own subdomain, under their own brand, with our name removed. Our closest feature
competitor, TeamingLabs, structurally cannot copy this — they license software to Spanish
agencies while bidding against those same agencies, served from their own domain.

> For event companies that run team-building as a business, RallyHub is the live event
> platform they can sell as their own, because it is white-label to the domain and we never
> appear in front of their client.

**The line:** *The platform, not the agency. We never show up at your client's event.*

**Order of messages:** white-label → the whole event in one tool → build once, rebrand per
client → the live control room → the store.

**Stop saying:** that the store is unique (TeamingLabs has it, deeper — already corrected on
the site) or that we invented the control room (espoto sells one at double the price).

## 3. Markets: three now, the rest funded by revenue

Nine markets is the destination, not the starting line. With one founder and under €500 a
month, three concentrated markets will produce more revenue in twelve months than nine
shallow ones.

### Phase 1 — months 1 to 4: Malta, Bulgaria, Poland

| Market | Why it is first |
|---|---|
| **Malta** | Home market. Fastest route to reference clients and a case study, and short enough to sell face to face. |
| **Bulgaria** | Founder speaks the language. mooveTEAM has localised here but their last product release was May 2025, so the incumbent is stale. |
| **Poland** | Largest of the nine by corporate base. No strong local platform. mooveTEAM is localised but weakly present. |

Founder sells all three personally. Malta and Bulgaria in person where possible, Poland
remotely in English, which its event industry handles comfortably.

### Phase 2 — months 5 to 9: Romania, Lithuania, Latvia

Opened with **commission-only reps**, recruited once Phase 1 has case studies to sell with.
Romania is the largest remaining market; the Baltics are small but Loquiz-adjacent, and
Loquiz has no live layer, which is the whole pitch there.

### Phase 3 — months 10+: Greece, Croatia, Cyprus

Seasonal, tourism-heavy MICE markets. Best entered after there is a repeatable rep playbook,
and best timed against their event seasons rather than our calendar.

**Deliberately not in this plan:** Spain (TeamingLabs owns the search, the relationships and
a 70% FUNDAE subsidy we cannot match) and Germany (espoto and Actionbound home turf). Revisit
only with white-label as the entire pitch.

**Decide:** whether Malta and Bulgaria alone are Phase 1, with Poland moved to Phase 2. That
would be the more conservative call, and defensible.

## 4. Revenue: where it actually comes from

The goal is revenue, so the plan needs to be explicit about which plan mix produces it.

Our own numbers, per agency, per month:

| Events per month | Pay Per Event | Starter (€20/mo) | Pro (€200/mo) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | €199 | **€169** | €299 |
| 2 | €398 | **€318** | €398 |
| 3 | €597 | €467 | **€497** |
| 5 | €995 | €765 | **€695** |

**What this means for marketing:**

1. **Starter beats Pay Per Event from the very first monthly event.** An agency running one
   event a month is paying us €30 too much on Pay Per Event. That is an easy, honest upgrade
   conversation and it converts free users into recurring revenue.
2. **Pro wins from three events a month.** That is the target state for a real agency.
3. Pay Per Event is the **acquisition tier**, not a revenue tier. It should be sold as the
   free way in, with the expectation of upgrading.

**The revenue engine is therefore:** free signup → free demo event → first paid event →
Starter → Pro. Marketing's job is to make step one loud and steps four and five obvious.

**Metric that matters most: events per agency per quarter.** An agency that runs one event
and never returns is not revenue. This is the number to watch above all others.

**Decide:** whether to show this comparison openly on the pricing page. It reveals that
Starter is cheaper than Pay Per Event, which loses a little margin on infrequent users and
buys a lot of trust and recurring revenue.

## 5. Channels, for a founder with 20 hours and no budget

Everything here is free or near-free. There is no paid advertising in this plan.

### 5.1 Founder-led outbound (the engine)

You are the first sales rep. This is where most of the 20 hours goes.

- **Build the target list:** every event agency, DMC and team-building company in the three
  Phase 1 markets. Realistically 50 to 200 each. Sources: Google Maps, national event
  associations, LinkedIn, conference exhibitor lists, and **mooveTEAM's own partner page**,
  which names 17+ agencies already buying this category.
- **The pitch is white-label**, not features. Second line is the free trial: build it and run
  a demo event before you pay anything.
- **Cadence:** email, follow-up, LinkedIn, call. Twenty to thirty new contacts a week is
  realistic at 20 hours, alongside demos.
- **Log every objection.** At this stage the objections are the most valuable marketing
  research available, and they are free.

### 5.2 The website, fixed to sell the free path

Highest-leverage work, and it costs only time. See section 1. Add the free CTA, rewrite
pricing to lead with the free path, and make the white-label claim prominent rather than a
line inside the branding section.

### 5.3 Review listings

We have zero reviews. Loquiz has 101 at 4.7 and adds more weekly. But **espoto, mooveTEAM and
Actionbound are all absent from Capterra and G2**, so the shelf is genuinely half empty.

- List on Capterra, G2 and GetApp. Free listings exist on all three.
- Ask the one or two existing clients this month.
- **Target: ten reviews before Phase 2 opens.**

### 5.4 Comparison pages

The cheapest converting content we can write, because the reader is already deciding.
"RallyHub vs Loquiz", "vs espoto", "vs mooveTEAM", "vs Actionbound". The competitive brief is
accurate enough to publish nearly as-is, and being scrupulously fair is what makes these
credible.

### 5.5 AI-assistant visibility

TeamingLabs publishes a detailed `llms.txt` telling assistants how to describe them,
including what not to invent. Nobody else in the category has noticed. **Roughly a day of
work, zero cost, and the best effort-to-visibility ratio on this list.**

### 5.6 Case studies from the existing clients

One or two informal clients is enough to start. Write them up properly: the brief, what was
built, how the event ran, what the client said. Two real case studies unblock Phase 2.

### 5.7 LinkedIn, founder-led

The only social channel worth the hours. Posts about how events actually run, what breaks,
what agencies get wrong. Not a brand account.

### 5.8 One small showcase, once Phase 1 has clients

TeamingLabs' best channel is an annual afternoon where agencies play the games as
participants. A ten-agency version in Valletta or Sofia is affordable and converts far better
than a deck. Phase 2, not now.

**Explicitly not doing:** paid search, brand advertising, conference booths, sponsorships,
agencies of record, or a marketing hire.

## 6. Localisation on this budget

Nine markets cannot all be localised now. The sequencing:

1. **English everywhere** as the working default and fallback
2. **Bulgarian and Polish** with Phase 1, since those are the markets being sold into
3. **Romanian, Lithuanian, Latvian** with Phase 2, done by the commission reps as part of
   their onboarding
4. **Greek and Croatian** with Phase 3

**What gets translated, in order:** the player-facing app first, then the marketing site,
then admin, then support docs. A participant hitting broken language mid-game costs the
agency their client; an admin screen in English does not.

**Rule:** never ship machine-translated player-facing text without a native speaker reading
it. At this budget that means reps, clients or personal contacts, not an agency.

## 7. Twelve-month calendar

### Months 1 to 2 — fix the funnel, gather proof
- Add the free CTA and rewrite pricing to lead with the free path
- Publish `llms.txt`
- List on Capterra, G2, GetApp; ask existing clients for reviews
- Write up the one or two existing clients as case studies
- Build the target lists for Malta, Bulgaria and Poland
- Publish the four comparison pages
- Start outbound in Malta

### Months 3 to 4 — sell Phase 1
- Outbound across all three Phase 1 markets
- Bulgarian and Polish localisation, player app first
- **Target: first paying agencies, ten reviews, three case studies**

### Months 5 to 9 — prove the rep model
- Recruit commission-only reps for Romania and the Baltics, armed with case studies
- One showcase afternoon in the strongest Phase 1 market
- Push existing free users toward Starter, and multi-event agencies toward Pro
- **Target: repeatable motion, recurring revenue from subscriptions rather than one-off events**

### Months 10 to 12 — widen
- Greece, Croatia, Cyprus via reps, timed to their event seasons
- Re-run the competitive brief
- **Target: revenue growing without the founder in every deal**

## 8. What we measure

**Leading**
- New free signups per week, and how many build a complete event (the real activation moment)
- Free users who run a demo event, and how many then run a paid one
- Outbound contacts, replies and demos booked per week
- Review count and rating

**Lagging**
- Monthly recurring revenue from Starter and Pro subscriptions
- **Events per agency per quarter** — the single most important number
- Paying agencies per market
- Devices per event, which drives the per-event fee

**Instrumentation must exist before Phase 2**, or Phase 2 is guesswork. We currently cannot
see how many people sign up, build an event, or run a demo. That is a bigger blind spot than
any channel decision in this document.

**Offline play:** the decision is to wait, so we must instrument the wait. Log every deal
where offline capability is raised as an objection. Revisit after ten logged instances or
six months, whichever comes first. Waiting without counting is how deals get lost quietly.

## 10. Risks

| Risk | Likelihood | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Nine markets dilute one founder | **High if we do not sequence** | Three markets in Phase 1, no exceptions |
| Free tier stays invisible | High today | Fix the site in month 1; it is the cheapest win here |
| Loquiz's review lead compounds | High if we do nothing | Ten reviews before Phase 2 |
| Commission reps under-produce | Medium | Test with one rep in one market before committing to three |
| espoto adds white-label or a store | Medium | Quarterly competitive re-check |
| Offline gap loses deals silently | Medium | Log every instance; revisit at ten |
| Founder capacity is the true ceiling | **High** | Revenue funds reps; do not open markets we cannot serve |
| Free users never upgrade | Medium | Make the Starter maths visible; watch events per agency per quarter |

## 11. If only three things happen

1. **Tell people the free path exists.** We already have what competitors use against us, and
   we hide it. Cheapest, highest-return change in this document.
2. **Get to three case studies and ten reviews.** Nothing scales without them, and the first
   two are already within reach.
3. **Make white-label the whole pitch.** The only thing we have that no competitor can
   answer, and our closest rival structurally never will.
