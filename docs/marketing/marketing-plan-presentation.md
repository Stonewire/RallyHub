# Marketing plan — presentation copy

Slide-ready text for Rumen to present to the team. First draft, framed for discussion.
Each block is one slide: **headline**, on-slide text, and a short *say this* note that is
not meant to appear on the slide.

---

## Slide 1 — Title

**Our marketing plan**
First draft · August 2026

*Say this: This is a draft, not a decision. I want us to leave this room having either backed it or changed it.*

---

## Slide 2 — What this meeting is for

We are not here to approve a plan.

We are here to answer five questions:

- Which countries do we go into first?
- How much are we willing to spend?
- What are we selling, in one sentence?
- What does success look like in twelve months?
- What are we deliberately not doing?

*Say this: If we walk out with those five answered, the plan writes itself. If we walk out with nine countries and no budget, we have achieved nothing.*

---

## Slide 3 — Where we actually are

Honest starting position:

- A product that works, and that we can demo live
- One or two clients, informally
- **Zero reviews. Zero case studies. Zero client logos.**
- A marketing site we rebuilt this month
- Under €500 a month to spend
- No sales reps yet, anywhere

*Say this: I want to start here because every plan that ignores its starting position fails. We are early, and the plan has to be built for early.*

---

## Slide 4 — What we sell, in one sentence

**RallyHub is the live event platform an agency can sell as their own.**

Their subdomain. Their logo. Their brand.
We never appear in front of their client.

*Say this: Everything else we do is a feature. This is the business.*

---

## Slide 5 — The one thing nobody else has

We looked at six competitors across Europe.

**Not one of them offers true white-label.**

Our closest competitor licenses their software to agencies while bidding against those same agencies for the same clients, on their own domain.

Their customers are visibly running a competitor's product.

*Say this: They can't fix this without giving up their main revenue. That's not a feature gap, it's a structural one. It's ours to take.*

---

## Slide 6 — Who we are up against

| | Country | What they are |
|---|---|---|
| **espoto** | Germany | Building the closest thing to our product |
| **Loquiz** | Estonia | Owns the search and the reviews |
| **mooveTEAM** | Spain | Chasing our exact buyer, the reselling agency |
| **TeamingLabs** | Spain | Closest feature match, but you cannot buy it |
| **Actionbound** | Germany | Huge reach, no live layer at all |
| **Cluetivity** | Germany | Sells city licences, not software |

*Say this: Six real competitors. Three of them should worry us. I'll take those three.*

---

## Slide 7 — The three that matter

**espoto** — the only one building our product. They already sell the live control room at double their base price. The market has proved it will pay for the thing we are betting on.

**Loquiz** — 101 reviews at 4.7. A blog twice a week. An instant free trial. Buyers find them, try them and decide before we exist to them.

**mooveTEAM** — aimed at exactly our buyer, and their pitch is "free to build, pay only when you sell". That is the sharpest attack on our pricing that exists.

*Say this: Notice none of these is beating us on product. They're beating us on being findable and being easy to start.*

---

## Slide 8 — We already solved this and never told anyone

mooveTEAM's best weapon is: **free to build, pay when you sell.**

Here is what a stranger can do on RallyHub today, with no card and no sales call:

- Sign up free
- Build their games
- Build and brand a full event
- **Run it live, for free**
- Pay €199 only when they run a real one

**We are better than their best weapon. Our website says "Book a demo" seven times and never says "start free".**

*Say this: This is the single cheapest win we have. It costs us a few hours of copywriting, not a quarter of engineering.*

---

## Slide 9 — Markets: nine is the destination, not the start

Where we want to be: Malta, Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, Bulgaria, Romania, Greece, Croatia, Cyprus.

**Nine countries, under €500 a month, no reps, one founder selling.**

Nine launches at once is nine half-launches.

*Say this: I'd rather be genuinely strong in three markets than a rounding error in nine. This is the part I most want us to argue about.*

---

## Slide 10 — Phase 1: three markets, months 1 to 4

**Malta** — home. Fastest route to a real reference client, and I can sell it face to face.

**Bulgaria** — I speak the language. The incumbent there hasn't shipped anything since May 2025.

**Poland** — biggest of the nine by corporate base. No strong local platform. Sells fine in English.

I sell all three personally.

*Say this: No reps yet. I'm the first sales rep, and that's the honest version of the next four months.*

---

## Slide 11 — Phases 2 and 3

**Months 5 to 9 — Romania, Lithuania, Latvia**
Opened with commission-only reps, recruited once we have case studies to hand them.

**Months 10+ — Greece, Croatia, Cyprus**
Seasonal MICE markets. Enter with a proven playbook, timed to their event seasons.

**Not in this plan: Spain and Germany.**
Spain is owned by two competitors and a 70% government training subsidy we cannot match. Germany is home turf for the two strongest players.

*Say this: Revenue from Phase 1 pays for the reps in Phase 2. We don't hire ahead of it.*

---

## Slide 12 — Where the money actually comes from

Our own pricing, per agency, per month:

| Events per month | Pay Per Event | Starter | Pro |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | €199 | **€169** | €299 |
| 2 | €398 | **€318** | €398 |
| 3 | €597 | €467 | **€497** |
| 5 | €995 | €765 | **€695** |

**Starter is cheaper than Pay Per Event from the very first monthly event.**

*Say this: An agency running one event a month on Pay Per Event is overpaying us by thirty euros. Telling them that is how we turn free users into recurring revenue.*

---

## Slide 13 — The revenue engine

Free signup → free demo event → first paid event → **Starter** → **Pro**

Marketing's job is to make step one loud and steps four and five obvious.

**The number that predicts revenue: events per agency per quarter.**

*Say this: An agency that runs one event and never comes back isn't revenue. Repeat usage is the whole business.*

---

## Slide 14 — How we sell it

**My time, 20 hours a week** — outbound to every event agency in the three markets. 50 to 200 targets each. Twenty to thirty new contacts a week.

**The website** — say "start free" as loudly as "book a demo".

**Review sites** — we have zero. Three of our six competitors also have zero. The shelf is half empty and we should take the space.

**Comparison pages** — vs Loquiz, vs espoto, vs mooveTEAM, vs Actionbound. The buyer reading those is already deciding.

**AI assistants** — one competitor publishes a file telling AI tools how to describe them. Nobody else noticed. One day of work.

**Case studies** — the one or two clients we already have.

*Say this: All of this is free or nearly free. The real budget is my hours.*

---

## Slide 15 — What we are deliberately not doing

- No paid advertising
- No conference booths or sponsorships
- No marketing agency
- No marketing hire
- No Spain, no Germany
- No nine-country launch

*Say this: Saying no to these is what makes the rest possible at this budget.*

---

## Slide 16 — Languages

Order, following the markets:

1. **English** everywhere, as the default and the fallback
2. **Bulgarian and Polish** with Phase 1
3. **Romanian, Lithuanian, Latvian** with Phase 2, done by the reps
4. **Greek and Croatian** with Phase 3

**Players first, admin last.** A participant hitting broken language mid-game costs our client their client. An admin screen in English costs nothing.

**Rule: no machine translation in front of players without a native speaker reading it.**

---

## Slide 17 — The first 90 days

**Month 1** — say "start free" on the site. Publish the AI file. List on Capterra, G2, GetApp. Ask our existing clients for reviews. Build the target lists.

**Month 2** — publish the four comparison pages. Write up our existing clients as case studies. Start outbound in Malta.

**Month 3** — outbound across all three markets. Bulgarian and Polish translation, players first.

**By day 90:** first paying agencies, ten reviews, three case studies.

---

## Slide 18 — What we measure

**Weekly**
- Free signups, and how many build a complete event
- Free users who run a demo event
- Outbound contacts, replies, demos booked

**Monthly**
- Recurring revenue from Starter and Pro
- **Events per agency per quarter**
- Paying agencies per market

**We currently cannot see any of this.** Fixing that comes before Phase 2, or Phase 2 is guesswork.

*Say this: This is the least exciting slide and probably the most important one.*

| | |
|---|---|

| Native-speaker translation review | €100 to €200 |
| Tools: outbound, analytics, email | €50 to €150 |

**The real budget is 20 hours a week of my time.**

*Say this: If we want to move faster than this, the conversation isn't about channels. It's about money or people.*

## Slide 20 — What could go wrong

| Risk | What we do about it |
|---|---|
| We spread across nine markets and win none | Three markets, no exceptions |
| The free tier stays invisible | Fix the site in month one |
| Loquiz's review lead becomes unassailable | Ten reviews before Phase 2 |
| Commission reps don't produce | Test with one rep before committing to three |
| espoto adds white-label | Re-check the competition every quarter |
| We lose outdoor events because we have no offline mode | Log every time it's raised; revisit at ten |
| I am the bottleneck | Revenue funds reps. Don't open markets we can't serve |

---

## Slide 21 — What I need us to decide today

1. **Markets** — three in Phase 1, or two?
2. **Budget** — is under €500 a month right, or do we fund this properly?
3. **Revenue target** — what number are we aiming at in twelve months?
4. **Free events** — unlimited, or capped before we ask them to upgrade?
5. **Pricing honesty** — do we publish that Starter is cheaper than Pay Per Event?
6. **Reps** — commission-only, and at what rate?

*Say this: I have a view on all six. I want to hear yours before I give mine.*

---

## Slide 22 — If we only do three things

1. **Tell people the free trial exists.** We already have what our competitors use against us, and we hide it.

2. **Get to three case studies and ten reviews.** Nothing else scales without them, and two are already within reach.

3. **Make white-label the whole pitch.** It's the only thing we have that no competitor can answer.

*Say this: If we do nothing else this quarter, do these three.*
