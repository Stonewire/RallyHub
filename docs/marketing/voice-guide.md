# RallyHub voice guide

One page. Applies to the marketing site, app copy, emails, socials, decks. Last updated 9 Aug 2026.

## The brief in one line

Internet-native direct. Reads young without cosplaying young. Works for a 28-year-old agency founder and a 45-year-old school coordinator.

## Rules

1. **Numbers over adjectives.** "About 10 minutes", not "incredibly fast". "6 ways to play", not "many game types".
2. **You over users.** Speak to the person running the event. Never "users", "clients can leverage", "organisations".
3. **Short sentences. Fragments allowed.** If a sentence has two commas, split it.
4. **Playful where the product is playful.** Store, bingo, winner reveals can joke. Pricing, privacy, data never joke.
5. **Honest caveats stay in.** The 10-minute claim always carries "once your games are built".
6. **No dated slang.** If a word needs a dictionary in two years, cut it. The voice ages well or it is a costume.
7. **British English.** No em dashes or en dashes anywhere.
8. **No italics in headings.** Bold weight and size carry emphasis.

## Banned words

synergy, solutions, empower, leverage (verb), seamless, innovative, revolutionary, cutting-edge, world-class, streamline, utilise, best-in-class, unlock (metaphor), supercharge, elevate

## Required habits

- Primary CTA is always **Book a demo**.
- Product nouns: event, run, stage, game, team, facilitator, big screen, player phones, event store.
- Say "web app" for install talk: runs in the browser anywhere, installable from the browser on your own devices.
- Branding story: "their logo on every screen", per-event client branding. Never "white-label" (branding removal is a paid option, not the default).
- Company = Stonewire Technologies. Product = RallyHub (rallyhub.games).

## Fixed distinctive assets (do not restyle per campaign)

- Charcoal #333333 + yellow #FFC107 + ivory/paper grounds
- The RallyHub wordmark and linked-people icon
- Slogan: "Stronger Teams, one game at a time."
- This voice

## Litmus test

Read it aloud. If it sounds like a person explaining something good to a friend at a bar, ship it. If it sounds like a website, rewrite it.
